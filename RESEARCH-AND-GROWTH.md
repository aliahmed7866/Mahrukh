# Mahrukh: a warmer shop, a more credible business

Research reviewed 30 September 2026. This is a practical plan for a small Pakistan-focused clothing business, not a forecast of sales or a claim that the website alone guarantees success. The recommendations below are our synthesis; observations from studies and museums are identified separately.

## The business proposition

**Pakistani clothing that feels personal, with enough detail to choose confidently.** Keep the warmth of the packaging: charcoal, antique gold, ivory, emerald and a welcoming voice. Make the buying information as considered as the visual identity. A customer should be able to answer: What do I receive? How will it fit? What is the fabric? When might it arrive? What happens if something is wrong? Who will help me?

Start with a small assortment that Mahrukh can photograph, measure, fulfil and support reliably. Five website categories do not require five fully stocked departments on launch day. Choose the first range using real supplier availability, margins and customer conversations. Do not buy stock just to fill an attractive grid.

These are useful capabilities, not a measured sales uplift.

## Culture with specificity and respect

Pakistan contains many languages, communities, climates and approaches to dress. Customers may prefer stitched outfits, unstitched fabric, abayas, contemporary silhouettes or a mixture. Age, city, household income and religious identity do not reliably tell us what one woman wants to wear. The store should support her choices rather than prescribe them.

The cultural notebook introduces four threads:

- **Ajrak/Ajrakh:** scholarly work connects this block-printing tradition with Sindh and Kutch, including resist/mordant processes and repeated geometry. [8]
- **Phulkari:** the Philadelphia Museum of Art situates this women-shaped embroidery tradition in Punjab across today’s Pakistan and India. Its historic examples are more varied than a single flower motif. [9]
- **Balochi embroidery:** the Textile Research Centre describes regional and cross-border connections, characteristic garment panels and both hand and machine embroidery. [10]
- **A Swat shawl:** a particular British Museum object offers a specific example of embroidered geometry and connections between neighbouring textile traditions. It is not a template for every garment from Swat. [11]

This is a starting point, not an exhaustive account of Pakistani culture. The new SVG studies are decorative original designs; they are not replicas, museum images, certificates of origin or proof that stock is handmade. A product described as Ajrak-inspired should not be sold as artisan-made Ajrak without evidence. Record the maker, location and technique where verified, obtain permission for names/stories/photos, agree compensation, and distinguish machine embroidery from hand work. Do not invent craft partnerships.

Use everyday life alongside celebration in the copy: work, study, meeting friends, family occasions and personal expression. Keep occasional Urdu touches, but do not imply the entire site is translated. Test a full Urdu experience with fluent readers before adding it; regional-language needs should be learned from customers rather than assumed. Avoid reducing Pakistani identity to bridal wear, a single province or compulsory modesty.

## Learn who the first customers are

Treat these as testable shopping needs, not fixed demographic personas:

| Need to investigate | What to learn | Website support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everyday and work/study dressing | Care effort, comfort, opacity, repeat wear and affordable complete-outfit cost | Everyday filter; lining, composition and care fields |
| Unstitched with a preferred tailor | Required fabric lengths/widths, patches, stitchability and tailoring budget | Exact supplied components and fabric dimensions; no fictitious body-size chart |
| A fixed-date celebration | Included pieces, finish, lining and realistic arrival timing | Occasion filter; product dispatch exception; visible policy links |
| Coverage and layering preferences | Desired lengths, sleeve dimensions, ease and inner layers | Actual measurements, XS–3XL when genuinely stocked, fit notes and helpful contact |

Recruit an initial 8–12 consenting prospective shoppers with different ages, body sizes, locations and clothing preferences, including people who do not usually buy online. This is a qualitative learning exercise, not a representative survey. Ask them to compare two real outfits, find a piece within their own budget, check a measurement, understand delivery and explain what would stop them ordering. Do not lead with “Do you like the design?” Record themes without unnecessary personal data. Pay participants fairly if recruiting beyond friends or existing customers.

Ask which language they want for product facts and support; what photographs they trust; which sizes are difficult to find; and whether they prefer tailoring. Bring unclear listing copy back to Mahrukh before spending on advertising.

## Assortment and merchandising

1. Begin with a manageable pilot, for example 8–12 thoroughly documented designs if supplier and cash capacity allow. This number is an operating suggestion, not a market benchmark.
2. Maintain a supplier sheet: purchase cost, verified fibre label, supplied pieces, technique, available sizes, lead time, defects procedure and replenishment reliability. Confirm every public craft or origin claim.
3. Photograph the exact SKU: front, back, embroidery/print close-up, lining and all supplied pieces. Keep colour treatment restrained. Include a flat photo when useful. Obtain model consent and image rights.
4. Measure each offered size; do not extrapolate all sizes from one sample. For unstitched fabric, list each panel’s length and width with units and explain separate patches. Add garment-specific care information.
5. Publish the total outfit price and transparent delivery. Only use a genuine, supportable previous price for discounts. No invented reviews, sold counters, countdowns or “bestseller” claims.
6. Plan seasonal buying against your actual delivery locations and observed demand. Test lighter or warmer assortments rather than treating Pakistan’s weather as uniform. Verify celebration dates and courier cutoffs near the event before advertising arrival promises.

## Service, payments and public launch

The customer promise should match operations. Publish response hours that one person can maintain. Confirm stock, size, address/city and full amount, then explain dispatch versus delivery. Use a courier agreement with clear COD settlement, failed-delivery, loss/damage and return terms. Record the reason for a cancellation or exchange with permission and avoid collecting sensitive details.

Existing COD, manual merchant transfer and order-specific hosted invoice workflows remain. They are not automated payment integrations. Enable only accounts and currencies approved for the real merchant. Never treat a WhatsApp screenshot as proof of settled payment; reconcile against the provider. See [BUSINESS-LAUNCH.md](BUSINESS-LAUNCH.md) for eligibility, verification, refunds and operational limits.

Before taking real orders, have a qualified Pakistan adviser review legal identity, registration, tax/invoice obligations and applicable provincial consumer requirements for the actual business. FBR’s Finance Acts index is a current starting point [12]; this project has not calculated tax liability or hard-coded historic e-commerce tax rates. A filled policy form is not a compliance certificate. Get the return/refund, defective-goods, complaints, privacy and retention wording reviewed for the business’s circumstances.

GitHub Pages remains a non-selling demonstration. Termux remains local testing on port 5050. Public selling needs an HTTPS Flask deployment with private persistent storage, backups, monitoring and the hosting controls in the launch guide. Keep the six fictional AI preview photographs out of live inventory.

## A practical acquisition and retention plan

Start with channels the seller can sustain. Use configured Instagram/Facebook/TikTok profiles to show real product details, a fit explanation, an outfit in motion and an honest dispatch update. Test English, Urdu or Roman Urdu captions with the intended audience. Do not assume one language reaches everyone.

For a four-post weekly pilot, try: one fabric/detail close-up; one fit demonstration; one everyday styling example; and one culture or maker story backed by permission and evidence. This cadence is a proposal to test, not a requirement to post daily. Link to the exact product or filtered collection rather than making people search again. Clearly disclose paid or gifted creator relationships; agree image usage in writing.

Use WhatsApp for help and order follow-up when the customer chooses it. Ask for separate permission before promotional broadcasts, state their frequency and honour opt-outs. Never scrape numbers, buy lists or reuse delivery details for unsolicited marketing. Invite a review after fulfilment, with consent, and publish only real feedback with permission. A small trusted customer base is more useful than fabricated social proof.

Organic search comes after the live shop exists: use descriptive, original product names and useful facts; keep guide links coherent; submit the real production domain to appropriate search tools once ready. Leave `noindex` on the fictional preview. Do not advertise the Pages link as a functioning store.

## Economics and learning loop

Calculate contribution before scaling spend. A practical starting model is:

`net sales excluding taxes/refunds + delivery collected − product cost − packaging − payment/COD fees − courier charges − exchange/RTO losses − attributable acquisition cost`

Avoid double-counting refunds and failed-delivery costs. Allocate the actual total costs of returned-to-origin parcels across the cohort you are assessing; do not assume every attempted COD order becomes revenue. Keep fixed overhead and owner time separately so positive contribution is not mistaken for profit. Ask an accountant about the treatment of taxes, inventory and cash settlement. Use actual supplier/courier quotes; no assumed industry margins are supplied here.

The new shop-health view shows pending requests, core listing completeness and totals **manually marked paid**, including delivery. These are not audited revenue or profit. There is no visitor analytics system, attribution, automatic gateway reconciliation or return-reason database in this release.

Keep a private weekly operating sheet with:

- Submitted, confirmed, dispatched, delivered/completed and cancelled orders by cohort; reconcile delivered figures with the courier.
- Delivery success = delivered parcels / dispatched parcels for a sufficiently matured cohort, with failed deliveries separately labelled.
- Exchange/return reasons, defect counts and fit questions by SKU/size; avoid drawing conclusions from one complaint.
- Contribution per fulfilled order and cash collected versus cash still due from COD/payment providers.
- First response time during published support hours and actual dispatch time versus the promise.
- Repeat purchase only where a customer is reliably and lawfully identified; shared devices and deleted cookies make browser counts unreliable.

If adding privacy-respecting visitor analytics later, define conversion as completed relevant actions divided by qualified sessions over a stated period. Current order counts cannot produce a conversion rate because this app does not count visits. Set experiment budgets from money the business can afford to lose; do not extrapolate six demo products into a market forecast.

## First 90 days

| Period | Work | Decision gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–30 | Interview prospective shoppers; select a small range; verify supplier facts; photograph and measure; review policies and merchant setup; test fulfilment end to end. | Real inventory, accurate listings and a working customer support/dispatch process before opening sales. |
| Days 31–60 | Run a small pilot through chosen channels; answer questions; reconcile payments; document delivery/fit issues; change confusing listings. | Reliable fulfilment and understood contribution before increasing spend or inventory. |
| Days 61–90 | Replenish only supported winners; test one message or assortment change at a time; review inclusivity and language needs; improve operations. | Scale only where repeat demand, capacity and economics support it. Pause weak designs or campaigns. |
